## 1. Information the prototype may process

Depending on how you use Vocogni and how the deployment is configured, the service may process:

- **Account and authentication information** needed to sign in, such as a name or email address provided through the authentication flow.
- **Profile and preference information** you choose to enter, such as grade, learning goals, preferred language, or display settings.
- **Learning records** such as selected courses, lesson progress, diagnostic answers, practice attempts, scores, and mistake tags.
- **Workspace content** such as notes, folder names, milestone titles, and checklist items you create.
- **Technical and operational information** processed by the app host, authentication provider, or database provider to provide and secure their services.

Avoid entering sensitive personal information unless the service specifically asks for it and the operator has explained the lawful purpose and handling.

## 2. Why the information is used

The prototype uses this information to authenticate a learner, restore saved state, display progress, provide supported rule-based learning recommendations, show the learner's notes and settings, and maintain and troubleshoot the application.

The operator should map every deployed data field and provider to a documented purpose before public launch, and should not collect information merely because it might be useful in the future.

## 3. Where information is processed

- **Auth0** is used for authentication.
- **Supabase/PostgreSQL** is used to persist learner state.
- The application runs on a hosting platform; the exact deployment configuration and associated technical logs must be documented by the operator.

The operator must confirm the provider terms, data regions, access controls, backups, subprocessors, retention periods, and any cross-border transfers for the actual deployment. Do not assume that all processing occurs in Indonesia.

## 4. The current assistant and AI processing

In the current implementation, the Vocogni Assistant uses ordinary application code and predefined, rule-based responses. It reads the profile, progress, settings, notes, and milestone values passed to it by the application. **It does not make a request to an external generative AI model in this build.** Its replies are not generated by a large language model.

That statement applies to the current source implementation, not automatically to every third-party service or future release. If an external AI model is added, the operator should update this notice before enabling it to explain the provider, what input is sent, the purpose, retention/training terms, transfer locations, safety limits, and any choice or consent mechanism required.

## 5. Sharing and disclosure

The current architecture necessarily relies on its authentication, database, and hosting providers to operate. The final notice must identify the actual providers and explain their roles and any other sharing that occurs. The operator should not promise that information is never shared or never leaves a country unless the deployed configuration and contracts support that promise.

## 6. Security and retention

The operator should apply appropriate access controls, keep credentials and secrets out of source code, limit internal access, and maintain a process for handling incidents. No internet service can promise absolute security.

Learner-state persistence is implemented in Supabase, but the retention schedule, backup-deletion timeline, and user-facing account/data deletion process have not yet been finalised for this prototype. These must be decided, implemented, and described before public release. Until then, do not promise a specific deletion deadline.

## 7. Your choices and data rights

Depending on applicable law and the actual deployment, users may have rights to request information about processing, access a copy of their data, correct it, request deletion or restriction, or withdraw consent where consent is the relevant legal basis.

The service must provide a working channel to make these requests and a documented process for responding. Use **[insert monitored privacy contact]**; this placeholder must be replaced before public release. The operator must also confirm how requests from parents/guardians and school administrators are handled without disclosing another learner's information.

## 8. Children and school use

The service is intended for learning, and learners may be minors. Before enabling public or school use, the operator must assess applicable child-data rules, guardian permission/consent requirements, age-appropriate notices, school responsibilities, and the need for any additional safeguards. Do not treat the existence of this draft as proof that those requirements have been met.

## 9. Changes to this notice

The published notice should show an effective date and version. Material changes in collected data, providers, AI processing, purposes, or retention should trigger an appropriate update and user communication before the changed processing begins, where required.

## 10. Legal reference for launch review

For Indonesia, have qualified counsel assess the Personal Data Protection Law (Law No. 27 of 2022), Government Regulation No. 71 of 2019 on Electronic Systems and Transactions, and any applicable rules for private electronic system operators against the actual service and deployment. This is a review prompt, not a legal conclusion about applicability or compliance.
